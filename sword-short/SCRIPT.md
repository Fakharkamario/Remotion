# How Sharp Can a Sword Get? (vertical short, target 52-56 s)

Narrator: "The Narrator" (curly top, round eyes, black hoodie, grey jeans), fully animated in a 3D cartoon world.

## Facts and sources
- Katanas come off the smith's anvil without a usable edge; a separate craftsman, the togishi (polisher), sharpens over ~1-3 weeks with progressively finer stones. (Nihonto polishing tradition; Kapp & Yoshihara, *The Craft of the Japanese Sword*)
- Tameshigiri test cuts use rolled tatami omote soaked in water; clean cuts leave the top section briefly resting in place.
- Thinner, more acute edges chip or roll on hard targets (edge geometry trade-off; basic metallurgy).
- Fractured obsidian edges measure ~3 nm across (often cited; used in some surgical scalpels).
- Aztec macuahuitl: wooden club edged with obsidian blades; obsidian is brittle and shatters against steel (Spanish conquest accounts).
- Single-atom-tip tungsten needles are made for field-ion / electron microscopy (e.g. NRC/NINT Canada, 2000s).
- Polishers deliberately leave a slightly convex "hamaguri-ba" (clam-shell) edge for toughness.

## Plain read for the voiceover
The sharpest thing ever made can't cut anything. So let's sharpen a sword till we find out why.
A smith forges a katana, quenches it, hands it over... and it's dull. Paper just bends.
The edge comes from someone else: a polisher. Weeks on one blade, with stones that keep getting finer.
A few stones in, it bites. Tomato? Halved.
Finish the polish, and it slices a soaked straw mat so cleanly, the top half just... sits there.
But keep going. Razor sharp: it splits a hair just by touching it.
Then you swing at bamboo, and the edge crumples like foil.
So, switch to glass. Obsidian breaks to an edge three nanometres wide.
The Aztecs lined real swords with it. Hit steel, and it shatters.
One step left. The sharpest thing humans have ever made is a tungsten needle. Its tip? A single atom.
Bump it into anything, and it's ruined.
That's why real polishers stop early. They round the edge on purpose, so it's less sharp than it could be.
Because the best sword in the world... isn't the sharpest one.
