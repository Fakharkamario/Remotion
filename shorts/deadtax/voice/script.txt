Before you die in Russia, you'd better check your bank account, because in the seventeen hundreds, being dead didn't exempt you from taxes.
When Peter the Great ran low on gold, he noticed a massive problem: peasants kept dying mid-year, instantly wiping their names off the tax rolls.
His solution was pure psychological warfare.
He declared that once your name was written on the official census, you were legally considered alive for tax purposes until the next census took place, which only happened every fifteen to twenty years.
That meant if your grandfather dropped dead in year one, your family was legally forced to pay his personal taxes for the next two decades as if he were still farming.
It got so absurd that wealthy aristocrats began secretly buying up the legal rights to thousands of dead bodies on paper, using ghost populations as collateral to take out massive bank loans.
You couldn't afford to live in Tsarist Russia, but you definitely couldn't afford to die.
Which is why for over a century, dying was the most expensive mistake a family could make.
