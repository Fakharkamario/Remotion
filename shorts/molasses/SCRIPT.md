# The Sweetest Disaster — Great Molasses Flood (30 s)

## Facts (sources: Stephen Puleo, *Dark Tide: The Great Boston Molasses Flood of 1919* (2003);
## Boston Globe archive; Sharp et al., "Modeling the Great Molasses Flood", Harvard SEAS / APS DFD 2016)
- 15 Jan 1919, ~12:40 pm, Purity Distilling Co. tank, 529 Commercial St, North End, Boston.
- Tank 50 ft tall, 90 ft wide, holding ~2.3 million US gallons (8,700 m³) of molasses.
- Tank leaked from the start; the company painted it brown to hide the leaks (Puleo).
- It was never tested by filling with water; it groaned, and witnesses heard rivets popping "like a machine gun".
- Wave up to 25 ft high, ~35 mph; it bent the girders of the elevated railway.
- 21 dead, ~150 injured. Weather jumped from ~2 °F to ~40 °F (−17 °C to 4 °C) the day before / that day.
- Harvard 2016 study: as the molasses cooled in the winter air, it grew far more viscous and trapped victims.
- Aftermath: court ruling (1925) led Massachusetts and then other states to require engineers to sign off on
  structural plans.
- Locals long claimed the North End smelled sweet on hot summer days for decades.
